# Week 3 - Visualization + Descriptive Statistics

## Overview
This week added the two tools that let you actually *see and summarize* the data you've been loading and cleaning since Week 2: visualization (Matplotlib, Seaborn, Plotly) and descriptive statistics (mean, median, mode, variance, standard deviation, distributions). The week closed by combining everything learned so far - from Week 2's cleaning skills through this week's charts and statistics - into a single structured Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) workflow.

## What We Covered

**Day 1 - Matplotlib Basics**
Covered the four core chart types: line charts (trends over a sequence), bar charts (comparing categories), histograms (the distribution of one numeric column), and scatter plots (the relationship between two numeric columns).

**Day 2 - Seaborn & Plotly**
Covered Seaborn for faster, more polished statistical charts (including box plots), correlation heatmaps to visualize how numeric columns relate to each other, pairplots to see relationships across many columns at once, and Plotly for interactive, hoverable/zoomable charts.

**Day 3 - Descriptive Statistics**
Covered the three measures of central tendency (mean, median, mode) and when each one is more trustworthy (especially with outliers), variance and standard deviation as measures of spread, and how to recognize normal vs skewed distributions - including using the relationship between mean and median to detect skew without even looking at a chart.

**Day 4 - Full EDA (Exploratory Data Analysis)**
Combined everything from Weeks 2 and 3 into one structured 7-step workflow: load and first look, handle missing values/duplicates, calculate descriptive statistics, visualize distributions, visualize relationships (heatmap/pairplot), compare categories (bar/box plots), and - critically - write plain-language observations connecting every chart and statistic back to what it actually means about the data.
